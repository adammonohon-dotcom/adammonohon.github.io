Adam Monohon

Adam Monohon is an art historian, critic, and curator specializing in the history and theory of photography, Chinese art, and nineteenth-century visual and material culture. He is a PhD candidate in Art History at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). (more)

His dissertation, Bohea Views: Photography, Landscape,and the Tea Trade in Late Qing Fujian, 1860–1888, reads photographs of Fujian by Lai Afong, John Thomson, and Tung Hing Studio in relation to the coastal province's booming nineteenth-century tea trade. Attending to issues of remediation, transcultural exchange, and geographical knowledge, it asks how mercantile capitalism and the medium of photography together reshaped the ways landscape was represented, understood, and experienced in late Qing China.

His doctoral research has been supported by the Bei Shan Tang Foundation, the Harvard-Yenching Library, the Museum of Fine Arts, Houston, and the Paul Mellon Centre for Studies in British Art, among others.

Monohon has held curatorial roles at the Center for Creative Photography, the J. Paul Getty Museum, and the Los Angeles County Museum of Art. Exhibitions organized include A/political Rocks (2023), Farewell Photography: The Hitachi Collection of Postwar Japanese Photographs, 1961–1989 (2022), and Edward Weston's Leaves of Grass (2019).
